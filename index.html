United Nations · Sustainable Development Goal 10

A fair chance should not
depend on where you start.

SDG 10 calls for reducing inequality within and among countries. That means widening opportunity, removing discrimination, sharing economic progress, and ensuring that people and countries have a meaningful voice.

02 / The global commitment

Reduce inequality within and among countries.

SDG 10 recognises that inequality is about more than income. It is also about unequal access to opportunity, services, rights, representation, and the power to influence decisions. Its targets address both disparities inside countries and gaps between them.

10 targets

One connected agenda: shared prosperity, inclusion, fair institutions, safe migration, and stronger international cooperation.

EQUAL OPPORTUNITY IN PRACTICE

Where you start should not decide how far you can go.

Access to quality education, decent work, health services, and a voice in decisions helps people take part in social and economic life.

Photo: Unsplash

The targets explained

From ambition to action.

These concise summaries follow the official United Nations wording. Select the source link below for the full target text and indicators.

10.1

Income growth

By 2030, sustain income growth for the bottom 40 per cent of the population at a rate higher than the national average.

10.2

Inclusion for all

Promote social, economic, and political inclusion regardless of age, sex, disability, race, ethnicity, origin, religion, or status.

10.3

Equal opportunity

Reduce unequal outcomes, eliminate discriminatory laws and practices, and strengthen equal opportunity.

10.4

Fairer policies

Use fiscal, wage, and social protection policies to progressively achieve greater equality.

10.5

Responsible finance

Improve the regulation and monitoring of global financial markets and institutions.

10.6

Representation

Strengthen the voice and representation of developing countries in international economic and financial institutions.

10.7

Safe migration

Support orderly, safe, regular, and responsible migration through planned and well-managed policies.

10.a

Fair trade treatment

Apply special and differential treatment for developing countries, particularly least developed countries, under WTO agreements.

10.b

Development finance

Encourage aid and financial flows towards countries where development needs are greatest.

10.c

Affordable remittances

By 2030, reduce remittance transaction costs to below 3 per cent and eliminate corridors costing more than 5 per cent.

WITHIN COUNTRIES

Fairer outcomes

Income, access to services, equal opportunity, and protection from discrimination.

AMONG COUNTRIES

A stronger voice

Fair participation in global decisions, trade, finance, and development.

MEASURING PROGRESS

Evidence matters

Disaggregated data helps reveal which groups are being left behind and whether policies work.

Targets describe commitments for governments and institutions. Progress depends on policy, resources, implementation, and reliable data—not individual goodwill alone.

03 / From understanding to change

Make equality part of the system.

Individual choices matter, but lasting change also requires institutions, employers, and governments to remove structural barriers and make fair outcomes possible.

DESIGN FOR EVERYONE

Inclusion becomes real when barriers are removed.

Accessible streets, transport, buildings, information, and public services allow more people to participate independently and with dignity.

Photo: United Nations

01

Listen to lived experience

Include people affected by a policy, service, or design in decisions from the beginning. Do not assume one person's experience represents an entire community.

02

Remove practical barriers

Make information, education, public spaces, transport, and digital services accessible. Review recruitment, pay, and progression for unequal outcomes.

03

Back fair rules and accountability

Support anti-discrimination protections, decent work, social protection, transparent institutions, and safe migration pathways.

A practical tool

Four questions for an equity check.

Use these questions when reviewing a school project, workplace, public service, or community decision.

  1. Access: Can people with different needs use it?
  2. Voice: Were the people affected involved in shaping it?
  3. Fairness: Could the same rule create unequal outcomes?
  4. Accountability: Is there a clear way to raise concerns and improve it?

Change happens at every level.

In everyday life

Challenge stereotypes, share access to opportunities, and make room for voices that are often ignored.

In organisations

Audit accessibility, pay, recruitment, representation, and the way decisions are made.

In public policy

Invest in fair services and social protection, enforce equal rights, and strengthen international cooperation.

The purpose behind our work

Why SDG 10?

SDG 10 is the United Nations commitment to reduce inequality within and among countries. It addresses unequal access to income, opportunity, services, representation, and participation.

Our project brings together visual stories and clear explanations to show why inclusion is not an optional extra. Fairer outcomes require listening to people affected by inequality and improving the systems that shape their choices and opportunities.
